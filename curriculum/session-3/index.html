Session 3

I Believe: Faith as Our Response

CCC 142–175

Slide 1 of 12

In this session

What is faith, and how is it different from just having an opinion? Faith is the first answer. At the Rite of Acceptance, inquirers are asked what they seek from the Church.

God speaks, and we answer. Out of the fullness of his love, the invisible God speaks to us as friends and invites us into his company. Faith is our fitting response: we freely entrust our whole selves, mind and will, to the God who reveals himself. Faith is not an opinion we hold; it is an answer we give to Someone.

At God’s word, Abraham leaves his homeland and sets out. His obedient trust makes him the “father of all who believe”. Mary’s whole life was a yes to God: “let it be done to me according to your word.”

Faith is first a personal adherence to God, and at the same time a free assent to the whole truth that God has revealed. It is a gift of God, a supernatural virtue, and an authentically human act. St Augustine: do not seek to understand in order to believe, but believe that you may understand.

Faith is necessary for salvation. We can lose it, so we nourish it by the Word of God, by prayer and by charity. In darkness and doubt, we look to the witnesses of faith: “I believe; help my unbelief.” Faith is personal, and it is never isolated. We say both “I believe” and “we believe”. The Church is the mother of all believers.

The slides ask who taught you to trust God, by words or by example, and where you find faith hard right now. They close with the Act of Faith.
