Purgatory: Why We Pray for the Dead

Why do Catholics pray for the dead?

Desktop: widescreen 16:9. Switch to Mobile for the portrait video.

Key points

Purgatory is not a second chance after a final no. It is God’s mercy, finishing the purification of those who die in his friendship.

  • All who die in God’s grace and friendship, but are still imperfectly purified, are assured of salvation; after death they undergo purification (CCC 1030).
  • The Church gives the name Purgatory to this final purification, which is entirely different from the punishment of the damned (CCC 1031).
  • The teaching is bound up with prayer for the dead, already in Scripture: Judas Maccabeus “made atonement for the dead, that they might be delivered from their sin” (2 Maccabees 12:46; CCC 1032).
  • From the beginning the Church has offered prayers for the dead, above all the Eucharistic sacrifice (CCC 1032, 1371, 958).
  • We remain in communion with the dead in Christ. Divination and consulting the dead are rejected (CCC 2116–2117). Family customs go to the parish priest.
